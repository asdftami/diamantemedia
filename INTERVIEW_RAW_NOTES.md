## ⭐ Project Insight (Computershare Loan Services)

**Q1. The problem before Project Insight**
- Computershare asked Tami to build a solution for its **internal investors**, who held **many portfolios of loans** in their investment profiles.
- Investors had to **manually request every data point** from Tami's stakeholders.
- Stakeholders then spent **3–5 days (up to a week+)** building **spreadsheets and PDFs** with the requested data.
- The process was **monotonous, repetitive, time-consuming and unintuitive** for investors, with heavy manual work on both sides.
- 📊 *Metric to use: data turnaround went from 3–5 days to ___ (find out what it became after).*

**Q2. Team and Tami's role**
- **Tami: the one and only UX designer** hired for the project.
- **Jeff, Project Manager**: "amazing" at scheduling and driving the project forward. Paired Tami with the stakeholder.
- **Stakeholder**: the person investors requested data from. *(Title TBD: Tami will check LinkedIn.)*
- **BSA (business systems analyst)**: started as an intern, hired permanently to work on this project with Tami. Wrote **SQL queries** to pull the data points investors requested, then organized them into **tables** so Tami could understand and sift through them and **populate the dashboards**.
- **Swati, main developer**, plus many other engineers.
- **Team size:** ~**12–15 people** in the **Investor Value Stream**, which built solutions for investors.
- The value stream had another project on **data feeds**. Tami wasn't heavily involved but learned from seeing the data.
- **Learning curve:** her **first finance/mortgage job**. She came from retail, sales and customer service, learned the domain **from the ground up**, and became well-versed.
- 💬 *Story angle: "the only designer on a 12–15 person team, in an industry I'd never worked in."*

**Q3. Discovery and research process**
1. **Kickoff with Jeff (PM)** to understand the problem.
2. **Assumption map**: listed her assumptions as questions, then got answers from Jeff.
3. **Stakeholder interviews** with the people who received investor requests directly.
4. **User interviews (indirect)**: Tami wrote interview questions, and stakeholders passed them to investors. Learned how investors used the data and which data points they needed.
5. **Persona** built from the interview findings.
6. **Artifact analysis**: studied many of the existing **spreadsheets and PDFs**.
7. **Dashboarding sessions** (workshops) with the stakeholder, BSA and project managers to turn data points into **graphical dashboard visualizations**.
8. ⭐ **Key pivot from research:** the plan was **one dashboard**, but research showed investors wanted **multiple dashboard views**, so Tami **added a secondary navigation** for the dashboards.
- *Artifacts to collect: assumption map, interview questions, persona, dashboard workshop outputs, before/after IA.*

**Q4. From one dashboard to many**
- **Original plan:** one **at-a-glance snapshot** dashboard with the data points the team *assumed* investors wanted most.
- ⭐ **Usability testing:** Tami ran **low-fidelity wireframe usability tests with real investors**. They kept asking for **data points the dashboard didn't have**, and the deeper the team dug, the more they found.
- **Resulting dashboard suite** (behind the secondary nav):
  - **Main dashboard** (overview)
  - **HELOC dashboard**
  - **Delinquency dashboard**
  - **Active and inactive loans dashboards**
  - **Disaster dashboard**, with an embedded **FEMA disaster risk map** giving investors **real-time insight** into how disasters affect their loans and portfolios ⭐ (standout feature)
  - *…and more (Tami will send screenshots of all the dashboards).*
- **Loan research search engine:** planned as the **next phase**. Tami made a **low-fi wireframe**, but the team **pivoted to prioritize the dashboards** and what each should show, so it stayed at low fidelity.
- *Artifacts to collect: screenshots of every dashboard, low-fi wireframes, usability test notes, loan search wireframe.*

**Q5. Outcome**
- **Did not launch.** Newrez acquired the company and **scrapped the project**: Newrez already had its own investor tool, so continuing wasn't worth it to them. "It was so freaking sad."
- During the acquisition things went quiet. After the cancellation, Tami moved to other value streams, including the **Israel Bond Center** work with the **Computershare Canada team** (*confirm*), then Newrez work.
- *Framing for the case study: an unlaunched project is normal and still strong. Show **validated design** (usability-tested with real investors) and **projected impact** (removing the 3–5 day manual turnaround), and end with an honest "what happened and what I learned" section.*

- ⭐ **Investor reactions in testing:** at every fidelity level they asked **"How can I access this?" / "When is this coming out? I need it now."** Lots of excitement and engagement.

**Q8. Prototype A/B/C: what won and why**
- **A** = the initial design (single dashboard, no secondary nav).
- After **1–2 usability tests**, investors wanted **more data at a glance**, and A didn't capture it. The team **asked investors directly which data points they wanted**, and those became the **secondary nav** introduced in **B and C**.
- **B vs C differed only in nav placement:** B had both navs on top (two levels); C had a left sidebar + top tabs.
